Start with the organization.

Build a shared picture of the client and their programs: legal identity, geography, operating history, funding needs, document links, contacts, and conversations. Keep unknowns visible instead of filling them with assumptions.

Project-specific context carries into research so you are not retyping the client’s need for every prospect.

A shortlist with reasons.

Explore independent funder research and connected Instrumentl matches in their own views. Use funder profiles to read saved source material, application routes, program information, and available IRS filing history.

Six-factor assessments show the score, the evidence coverage, and what still needs discovery. Current and future-cycle leads remain distinguishable. Optional open-chat research uses a prepared packet; the app does not run an unattended model job.

Keep the ordinary work close.

Plans, tasks, and dates

Daily plans, a work board, calendar, checklists, owners, and follow-up tasks keep the next action visible.

A conversation with context

Threaded project discussions, questions, activity, personal unread counts, and record history help colleagues stay caught up.

Application preparation, reusable content, and client follow-up live alongside research. Connected external actions follow the app’s separate permission and approval rules; a research score is not permission to apply or change an outside account.

A report worth opening.

Build a visual client report with a ranked shortlist and linked, self-contained funder profiles. Include the saved fit narrative, timing, funding routes, findings, and source links rather than handing over a wall of unexplained scores.

The report-and-spreadsheet ZIP keeps the portable HTML and linked Excel workbook together. Extract both files into the same folder. The saved report can be read without an app login; following external source links requires internet access.

A closed round can still be a good future lead. Keep the timing qualification, the next action, and any published dates with it. Unknown future dates stay unknown.

Local app. Shared context.

Each person runs the app on their own computer and signs in with their own Google account. Workspace records sync through a Google Drive folder shared by the team. Work can be saved locally while offline; sharing resumes when the connection returns.

This public site is the product and support home. It does not host your client workspace. The app is a private beta, and Windows onboarding, connected actions, and live team workflows still need real-world validation before a 1.0 claim.
