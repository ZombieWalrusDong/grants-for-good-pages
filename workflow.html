How it works

Research that adds up.

Know the client. Find the strongest prospects. Settle the questions that change a decision. Hand over something your colleague can use.

On this pageClient contextThree research passesThe six-factor rubricHuman decisionsThe handoff

Begin with the client and the need.

Record who can apply, who the program serves, where it operates, what needs funding, and what is still unconfirmed. A draft intake produces provisional findings. Confirm the important facts before treating a match as ready to pursue.

Three passes. A clearer shortlist.

1

Screen broadly

Review the available project evidence across the selected matches. Separate strong candidates, questions worth resolving, future cycles, and weak leads. This pass uses the packet’s evidence without new browsing.

2

Verify the deciding questions

Open current funder guidance for the promising routes. Confirm applicant eligibility, geography, permitted costs, application access, and timing. Spend time on uncertainties that could change the recommendation.

3

Research the finalists

Build a fuller picture of the funder, route, fit, calendar, and remaining qualifications. Preserve a readable rationale and source trail, then bring the findings into the team’s review and client report.

Research runs in your open chat, when you ask for it.

The app prepares assignments and imports structured results. It keeps the saved findings and progress, but it does not secretly run a model in the background. Human review stays separate from importing suggestions.

A score needs context.

The six-factor rubric rates known criteria from 1 to 5. Unknowns stay unknown. Only the known criteria contribute to the reweighted score, and coverage tells you how much of the rubric has actually been assessed.

The six-factor rubric
CriterionWeightThe question
Mission & program25%Does this work belong in the funder’s stated interests?
Eligibility & geography25%Can this applicant and service area qualify?
Comparable giving20%Is there evidence of relevant past support?
Funding value15%Is the likely support worthwhile relative to the work?
Feasibility10%Is the application route practical?
Relationships5%Is there a known, useful connection?

A high score with little evidence is not the same as a well-supported recommendation. Read coverage, confidence, eligibility, reasons, and sources together. Eligibility limits can cap a score; an evidenced contradiction with a published rule can disqualify a route.

Timing is a separate question. A closed application window is not, by itself, a permanent disqualification. Keep a good future lead and record when to check again—without guessing next year’s deadlines.

Review, decide, and record the next step.

Suggestions are not human approval. Review the findings, make the recommendation your own, and record a clear next step: pursue, resolve a question, revisit a future cycle, or set the route aside.

Where an external connection is configured, any action in another service must pass its separate authorization rules. Importing research does not submit an application, contact a funder, or authorize an Instrumentl change.

Make a useful handoff.

Choose the researched routes that belong in the client report. Keep promising future prospects alongside current options, with the qualification and calendar information visible. Use the visual report for the first read and the linked funder profiles for depth.

The source trail and earlier detailed research remain useful. A broad rescreen does not make an earlier funder dossier disappear; check its age and confirm any rule that may have changed.
