A familiar mark, with room to grow.

grants forgood.

The green g. mark carries forward the identity already used in the app. A small yellow detail brings a little warmth; a straightforward wordmark keeps the name easy to recognize.

Give the mark breathing room. Keep its shape and colors intact, use a clear background, and do not place it over a busy image. Do not imply an endorsement by another organization.

Use forest for primary text and strong surfaces. Fresh green is a supporting highlight, not a text color on white. Paper leaves room for dense research to breathe.

Headings use Georgia, with a system sans serif for reading and interface text. No external font request is needed. Pair editorial clarity with practical, readable labels.

Good work, well supported.

Write like a capable colleague. Say what was found, why it matters, what is uncertain, and what happens next. Prefer a useful sentence to an unexplained badge.

Avoid inflated promises, invented impact numbers, claims of guaranteed grant wins, and language that turns a suggestion into approval. Let the source and the work speak for themselves.
